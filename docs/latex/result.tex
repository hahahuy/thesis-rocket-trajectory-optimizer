\chapter{Results}

\section{Evaluation Setup}

This chapter evaluates the performance of the proposed PINN-inspired physics-regularized 
neural surrogate uing a held-out test set that was not used during training. To evaluate 
how well trajectories are reconstructed, examine numerical error patterns alongside adherence 
to selected physical constraints.

The evaluation set includes several numerically generated ascent trajectories for rockets employing 
the same high-fidelity numerical integration framework described in Chapter II. Every 
path over a set period of thirty seconds, including movement-related conditions, is part 
of the evaluation set. States of rotation are described through quaternions, while 
changes in vehicle mass unfold over time. All trajectories are sampled at a 
uniform temporal resolution of 50 Hz, consistent with the training setup.

Prediction accuracy is evaluated by comparing the PINN outputs against the reference 
solver outputs at corresponding time instances. Absolute error is computed 
pointwise in time for representative trajectories, while root-mean-square 
error is used to quantify performance across the entire test set. All reported 
RMSE values are computed per trajectory and then aggregated across the test 
dataset.

No control inputs or reference trajectories are provided to the network during 
inference. The model predicts the full state evolution solely from time and mission 
context parameters, without access to control inputs or reference trajectories during 
inference.

\subsection{Mass evolution}

The mass evolution results highlight the model's ability to approximate the overall trend of propellant 
consumption during the burn phase and the constant-mass regime after cutoff, as 
shown in Figure~\ref{fig:mass_evolution}. Small oscillations around the terminal mass, visible in 
the figure, indicate limitations of soft constraint enforcement rather than numerical instability.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=14.5cm]{figures/evaluation_report/figures/figure_3_3_mass_evolution.png}
    \caption{Mass evolution: Compares the predicted mass evolution against the reference solution. During the

    model's output shows slight deviations near the endpoint. These differences persist without

\subsection{Horizontal motion}

The horizontal motion results illustrate small deviations from the zero-motion reference trajectory, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:horizontal_position}. These deviations reflect imperfect symmetry preservation and provide insight into the limitations of penalty-based constraints in unconstrained dimensions.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=14.5cm]{figures/evaluation_report/figures/figure_3_4_horizontal_position.png}
    \caption{Horizontal position: Shows how position changes sideways. In both left-right directions, the target path stays 
    flat at zero. Instead, the forecasted route shifts slightly, showing movement off course. This 
    shift appears because weak constraints affect symmetric systems more strongly. Sensitivity grows 
    when balance rules lack tight control.}
    \label{fig:horizontal_position}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Attitude representation}

The attitude results demonstrate stable quaternion behavior throughout the trajectory, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:attitude_representation}. Explicit normalization effectively prevents drift in the orientation representation, ensuring physically meaningful rotational states, which is confirmed by the extremely small deviation from unity observed in the figure.

\begin{figure}[htbp]

\section{Constraint Satisfaction Results}

This section examines whether the model's predictions satisfy key physical constraints imposed during 
training. By plotting the time evolution of the physics residual norm, we can see how well the model's 
predictions satisfy the governing equations of motion.

\subsection{Physics residual norm}

Figure~\ref{fig:Physics_residual_norm} shows the time evolution of the physics residual norm for the representative trajectory. The residual magnitude varies over time and exhibits a notable decrease toward the end of the trajectory, as shown in the figure. The reduction in residual magnitude should not be interpreted as uniform improvement in physical accuracy, but as a consequence of reduced actuation and simplified dynamics after thrust termination.

\begin{figure}[htbp]

\section{Summary of Observed Results}

In this chapter, we demonstrate that the proposed neural surrogate is capable of 
reconstructing the overall structure of rocket trajectories, as shown by close visual agreement 
in vertical position and velocity in Figures~\ref{fig:vertical_motion} and~\ref{fig:vertical_velocity}. 
Quantitative evaluation across the test set, as summarized in Table~\ref{tab:per_state_rmse} and 
Figure~\ref{fig:RMSE_distribution}, indicates that prediction errors vary by state type, with 
mass and rotational states exhibiting distinct error distributions. Quaternion normalization was maintained 
throughout the trajectory via constant satisfaction of the quaternion norm as shown in 
Figure~\ref{fig:attitude_representation}, while mass monotonicity violations occur in the predicted 
trajectories, as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:mass_monotonicity}. The physics residuals 
remain bounded and decrease toward the end of the simulated time horizon, as shown in 
Figure~\ref{fig:Physics_residual_norm}.

All observations reported in this chapter are based solely on empirical evaluation results 
without interpretation of underlying causes.
