\section{Background and Motivation}

Trajectory optimization for rocket ascent is a foundational problem in aerospace engineering, involving 
the determination of thrust and steering profiles that guide a launch vehicle from liftoff to a desired 
terminal state while satisfying nonlinear dynamics, operational constraints, and structural limits. Classical 
solution approaches are well established and include indirect methods derived from Pontryagin’s Maximum 
Principle as well as direct transcription techniques such as collocation and pseudospectral methods, which 
convert the continuous-time optimal control problem into a large-scale nonlinear programming 
problem~\cite{bryson1975,ross2004,betts2010}.

While these methods are theoretically rigorous and widely used, they rely on repeated numerical integration 
of high-dimensional nonlinear ordinary differential equations and the solution of constrained optimization 
problems with thousands of decision variables. As a result, they can become computationally expensive in 
repeated-evaluation settings, sensitive to initialization, and less amenable to direct embedding in higher-level 
loops when repeated evaluation or gradient access is required within higher-level design 
loops, uncertainty analysis, or real-time decision-making frameworks. These limitations become particularly 
pronounced when repeated trajectory evaluations or gradient information are required, such as in design 
space exploration or optimization under uncertainty.

To address these challenges, various surrogate modeling strategies have been investigated as practical alternatives 
for approximating rocket dynamics while lowering computational demands. There are many different common methods used 
to increase the speed of simulations, including; (1) Response Surface Methods, (2) Reduced Order Models, and (3) Fully 
Data-driven Neural Networks. Many times these methods provide enormous improvements in simulation speed compared to 
traditional approaches; however, these same methods have also been shown in a number of cases to lack robustness and 
therefore do not provide sufficient assurance that what is predicted outside the training conditions will be physically 
reasonable.

Meanwhile other techniques under development within the field of Scientific Machine Learning have led to the emergence of 
"Physics-Informed Neural Networks", and more broadly a number of other types of Physics Regularized Learning Models, all of 
which use physical knowledge during the training of a model. Instead of simply fitting a model to observed data, these methods 
utilize additional constraints to allow the model to take on additional information from the governing physics equations 
governing system behaviour. This formulation allows Neural Networks to maintain their flexibility while being guided by both 
observed data and the underlying physical laws that govern all physical systems. The rapidly growing intersection between 
Surrogate Modelling techniques and Physics-Informed learning techniques, therefore, provides an ideal impetus to re-evaluate 
Rocket Ascent Modelling through the use of a Machine Learning lens.
\subsection{State of the Art}

\noindent In this thesis, the term PINN-inspired is used to denote physics-regularized learning 
architectures that incorporate physical structure through soft constraints, rather than 
strict equation enforcement.

\noindent Physics-informed neural networks were originally proposed as a means of solving forward and 
inverse problems governed by differential equations by embedding the governing equations 
directly into the learning objective. Since their introduction, PINNs have been applied to a 
wide range of problems in fluid dynamics, solid mechanics, and control, and extensive surveys 
have documented both their potential and their limitations. In particular, prior work has 
highlighted challenges related to optimization stiffness, loss balancing, long-horizon 
accuracy, and constraint enforcement~\cite{raissi2019,wang2022}.

Analytical and empirical studies have further shown that PINN performance is highly sensitive 
to architectural choices, training strategies, and the relative weighting of data and physics 
losses. While PINNs can approximate smooth solution manifolds effectively, they do not 
generally provide hard guarantees of constraint satisfaction when physical laws are 
enforced through penalty-based formulations~\cite{wang2022}. These limitations are particularly pronounced 
when the governing equations are nonlinear and coupled, as is the case for rocket ascent dynamics.

In aerospace applications, learning-based surrogate models have traditionally been used for 
aerodynamic prediction, reduced-order dynamics, or narrow operating regimes. The application 
of physics-informed learning to trajectory optimization problems remains comparatively limited, 
particularly for six-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) rocket ascent dynamics, which involve strong 
nonlinearities, coupling between translational and rotational motion, and mass depletion 
effects.

Existing studies in this area have primarily focused on forward or inverse dynamics problems, while 
fewer works investigate structured neural surrogates trained on optimal trajectories as approximations 
to high-fidelity numerical integration. This gap is especially relevant for ascent problems, where repeated 
trajectory evaluation is computationally demanding and where physical structure is well understood but 
difficult to enforce strictly within learning-based models~\cite{raissi2019,wang2022}.

\subsection{Rationale}

The central rationale of this thesis is to investigate whether a structured, PINN-inspired, 
physics-regularized neural network can serve as an effective surrogate for rocket ascent 
trajectory reconstruction. Rather than learning a closed-form state-transition function or 
directly optimizing control inputs, the proposed approach aims to approximate optimal ascent 
trajectories as explicit functions of time and mission parameters.

Surrogates that ascertain the approximate behavior of a trajectory can be generated quickly and 
differentiably (allowing gradients to be computed) by utilizing the computational load during the 
offline training stage rather than performing the numerical integration task at each iteration during 
the inference stage. In addition, to provide further opportunities for physical interpretability, 
physics-based regularization is performed by incorporating 'soft' residual penalties (in fact, the 
residuals are derived from a reduced form of the governing equations) to ensure that the approximate behavior 
produced by the surrogate is both physical plausible and retains the flexibility to be modified through 
the data-driven learning process.

This formulation is particularly suited to ascent analysis and preliminary design studies, 
where fast evaluation and smooth sensitivity information are valuable, but strict enforcement 
of all physical constraints may be relaxed. The surrogate is not intended to replace the 
optimal control solver, but to approximate its output trajectories under similar conditions. 
The study therefore focuses on assessing the accuracy, stability, and physical plausibility of 
trajectory reconstruction under soft constraints, rather than on direct deployment in 
closed-loop control or safety-critical applications.

\subsection{Problem Statement}

The problem addressed in this thesis is the development of a PINN-inspired, physics-regularized 
trajectory surrogate capable of approximating three-dimensional rocket ascent behavior during 
the launch phase. The surrogate is trained on full-state trajectories generated from high-fidelity 
numerical simulations of a 6-DOF rigid-body dynamics model and is designed to reproduce the dominant 
coupled translational, rotational, and mass-depletion characteristics of ascent under soft physics-based 
regularization.

While the broader motivation is to support future trajectory optimization workflows, the scope of 
this study is limited to trajectory reconstruction and physical consistency analysis, rather than the 
direct computation of optimal control inputs using the learned surrogate.

\subsection{Objectives}

The specific objectives of this thesis are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item To formulate a three-dimensional rocket ascent model suitable for
        physics-regularized learning.
  \item To design a structured PINN-inspired architecture incorporating Fourier time
        embeddings, context encoding, and residual multilayer perceptrons.
  \item To train the surrogate using full-state trajectory data generated from 
        high-fidelity numerical simulations relative to the surrogate model.
  \item To evaluate the accuracy, stability, and approximate physical consistency of the
        learned surrogate during the launch phase under soft physics-based constraints.
  \item To assess, at a conceptual level, the suitability of the surrogate as a building block
        for potential future integration into constrained trajectory optimization frameworks,
        once extended to explicitly condition on control inputs and enforce the full controlled dynamics.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope and Limitations}

The scope of this work is limited to three-dimensional rocket ascent dynamics
without wind disturbances. The surrogate is trained on trajectory data covering
the initial 30~seconds of flight, corresponding to the launch phase. As a
result, conclusions regarding full-ascent modelling, closed-loop control
optimization, and generalization beyond the training regime are outside the
scope of this thesis.

In particular, the study does not demonstrate closed-loop optimization of
control inputs using the learned surrogate. These limitations are acknowledged
and discussed as directions for future work.

\subsection{Research Framework}

The research framework follows a multi-level structure. At the data generation
level, high-fidelity numerical simulations and optimal control solvers are used
to produce dynamically feasible ascent trajectories. At the learning level,
these trajectories are used to train a PINN-inspired, physics-regularized
neural surrogate that approximates state evolution over time. At the application
level, the trained surrogate is evaluated as a candidate approximation to
numerical integration in future optimization and guidance workflows, rather than
as a full replacement for a high-fidelity dynamics model.
