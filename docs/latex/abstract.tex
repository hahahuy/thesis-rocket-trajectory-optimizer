    \textbf{ABSTRACT}
\end{center}
\vspace{-0.3cm}
\doublespacing
\justifying
This thesis investigates PINN-inspired neural surrogates for six-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) launch vehicle ascent dynamics, 
with the goal of approximating trajectories generated by a high-fidelity solver, at substantially reduced computational cost. 
Starting from a rigid-body 6-DOF dynamics model and a direct optimal control formulation serving as a reference trajectory generator, 
a dataset of numerically optimal ascent trajectories is generated using collocation-based methods. These reference trajectories serve as 
supervision for training structured physics-informed regularization neural surrogates that predict full state evolution from time and mission 
context parameters.

The primary contribution of this work is the design and evaluation of a structured physics-based regularization neural surrogate architecture, 
referred to in this thesis as Direction AN, which combines Fourier time embeddings, a context encoder for physical parameters, a shared 
residual backbone, and specialized output branches for translational motion, rotational dynamics, and mass depletion. Physical structure 
is encouraged through soft physics-based regularization derived from a reduced, uncontrolled form of the equations of motion, rather 
than strict enforcement of full controlled dynamics, together with additional structural constraints such as quaternion normalization, 
mass monotonicity, and boundary consistency. A phased training strategy is employed to balance data fidelity, physics regularization, 
and smoothing terms, improving stability during learning.

The resulting surrogate accurately reconstructs the dominant qualitative and quantitative structure of the state trajectories over the launch phase (0–30 s) across 
a range of sounding-rocket configurations, while maintaining approximate physical plausibility under soft constraints relative to a high-fidelity 
truth integrator. Although the proposed model is not employed to guide or optimize control inputs in the present study, its differentiable structure 
and physics-regularized formulation conceptually suggest potential suitability for future integration into gradient-based trajectory optimization 
frameworks, provided that the architecture is extended to explicitly condition on control inputs and to enforce the full controlled dynamics. 
Results indicate that constructing a neural surrogate with precise characteristics of physical phenomena allows for the recreation of rocket 
ascent data in a way comparable to actual data collected via rocket launches. This will provide the capability to analyse launch trajectories 
quickly and affordably while providing opportunities for improvement of said trajectories.
